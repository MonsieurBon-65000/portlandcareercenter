![alvaro-serrano-133380](/images/alvaro-serrano-133380.jpg) As you dive deeper into your job search, you may consider going out on your own. You are not alone. It is expected that within the next decade more than 50% of U.S. workers will be freelancers or self-employed. You may be ready to join this army of freelancers, but before taking the leap, there are a few things you MUST consider.

## 1) Who are your customers and what do they need?

## 2) What** **will you sell them and how much will you charge?

## 3) How much income do you need in order to make a living?

Let’s look at each question a bit more closely.

## 1) FOCUS ON CUSTOMERS’ NEEDS

Every business person knows that the business is not really about the business. Successful businesses take careful measure of who their customers are and what needs, wants and/or desires they have that the business can address. Getting a clear picture of who you will serve in your business is a critical first step. The alternative, of course, is to create something that people neither want nor need. This is a surefire way to waste time and money.

The best way to determine the needs of your potential customers is to create a customer persona. This tool will help you identify the things that motivate your customers and keep them up at night or get them out of bed in the morning.

## 2) IDENTIFY WHAT YOU OFFER THAT CUSTOMERS ARE WILLING TO PAY FOR

Once you know the needs, wants and desires of your customers, you can determine your product or service offerings.

Begin by creating a product or service map which includes offerings at a variety of price points. Each item on the map should be tied directly to a need you have identified in your customer persona. Look at ways you can use the products and services to lead people from prospect to customer to high paying customer. What is the typical journey your customers will take, from unaware that you exist to becoming an evangelist for your products and services?

## 3) KNOW YOUR TAKE HOME NUMBER

There is a difference between money you make ![rawpixel-com-252130](/images/rawpixel-com-252130.jpg)by charging for your product or service and money that you take home in your paycheck. There is a cost to doing business and savvy business owners know how to manage costs.

If you are making a product there will be costs in raw materials and production. These are commonly called “Costs of Goods Sold.” You will make more at the end of the day if you track what it costs to make your products and research ways to reduce those costs.

Your business will also have expenses that are not directly related to your products or services. Overhead expenses things like rent, marketing, and utilities such as your phone. Successful freelancers are always on the lookout for ways they can reduce overhead without reducing quality.

Money left over after you have deducted your costs and overhead is profit. Your paycheck (and taxes) come out of this profit, so plan accordingly. Many self-employed people make the mistake of thinking that the money they bring in from sales is the money they have to live on. If you don’t calculate your expenses, you may be shocked when you find out you don’t have any money left to live on.

Freelancing, consulting and self-employment may a great option for your next career move, but don’t jump into it without first considering these key things. To be successful you must know your customers, what you plan to sell to them, and how much money you need to make.
